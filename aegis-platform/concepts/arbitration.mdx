Security tools and cost tools answer the *same* question — "what is the right state for
this resource?" — and disagree. Nobody arbitrates. AEGIS makes that arbitration the
product.

## The model: a constrained multi-objective problem (CMOP)

The kernel takes two inputs:

- **Proposals** (from the FinOps agent): each has an `id`, a `resource`, a
  `monthly_savings_usd`, and a flag for whether applying it `reduces_control_coverage`.
- **Obligations** (from the Compliance agent): each binds a `resource` with a `control_id`
  and a `severity` — `Mandatory` or `Soft`.

It optimizes two objectives at once:

| Objective | Direction |
|---|---|
| Monthly savings | maximize ↑ |
| Coverage loss (proposals that erode control coverage) | minimize ↓ |

## The veto gate (hard constraint)

A **mandatory** obligation is a hard veto. A proposal is infeasible if it reduces control
coverage of a resource bound by a mandatory obligation:

```text
veto(proposal) ⇔ proposal.reduces_control_coverage
                 ∧ ∃ obligation. obligation.severity = Mandatory
                                ∧ obligation.resource = proposal.resource
```

This is enforced identically in two places that must agree: the Rust kernel
([`is_vetoed`](https://github.com/tranmyphuc/aegis/blob/main/core/kernel/src/lib.rs)) and the
OPA/Rego policy ([`policy/arbitrate.rego`](https://github.com/tranmyphuc/aegis/blob/main/policy/arbitrate.rego)).

## The Pareto frontier (the answer)

Vetoed proposals are removed, then the kernel enumerates feasible subsets and keeps only
the **non-dominated** plans. A plan `a` *dominates* `b` when it is at least as good on
every objective and strictly better on at least one:

```text
dominates(a, b) ⇔ a.savings ≥ b.savings ∧ a.coverage_loss ≤ b.coverage_loss
                 ∧ (a.savings > b.savings ∨ a.coverage_loss < b.coverage_loss)
```

The result includes:

- the **frontier** of feasible, non-dominated plans (best-savings first);
- the **recommended** plan (highest feasible savings);
- the **veto rationale** — *why* the cheaper plan lost;
- the **savings forfeited** to honor mandatory obligations.

<Note>
Subset enumeration is exponential, so it's capped at 20 proposals. Beyond that, use the
**weighted-scalar fallback** (`arbitrate --strategy weighted`), which collapses the two
objectives into one tunable score and runs in linear time.
</Note>

## Worked example

200 idle E5 seats: 160 in a general cohort, 40 in a regulated cohort that A.8.15 requires
to retain E5-tier audit logging.

| Proposal | Savings | Erodes coverage? | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reclaim-e5-general` | $9,120/mo | no | ✅ applied |
| `reclaim-e5-regulated` | $2,280/mo | yes | ✗ vetoed by **A.8.15** |

The recommendation reclaims the general cohort for $9,120/mo and forfeits $2,280/mo to keep
the regulated cohort compliant — and it says so explicitly. Run it yourself in the
[Quickstart](/aegis-platform/quickstart).
